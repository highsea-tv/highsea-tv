# 0051. The playback engine: direct play, then direct stream, then HLS transcodes made on demand

- **Status:** Accepted (2026-10-27, at checkpoint 4.1b's sign-off)
- **Date:** 2026-10-23

Drafted at Phase 4's checkpoint 4.0b ([phase-4-checkpoint-0.md › ADR drafts](../plans/phase-4-checkpoint-0.md#adr-drafts-40b)). It becomes Accepted at 4.1, which builds it, with a dated section for whatever building it changes. Hardware acceleration has its own ADR at 4.3.

## Context

Phase 4 plays films ([phase-4-clients.md › Playback on the server](../plans/phase-4-clients.md#playback-on-the-server)). highsea's files are whatever the owner's releases are: MKV and MP4, H.264, HEVC and AV1, 8- and 10-bit, HDR10 and Dolby Vision, DTS, TrueHD, AAC and AC-3, with SRT, ASS and PGS subtitles. Its clients are a browser, a Tauri webview, AVPlayer on Apple's platforms and Media3 on Android, and each plays a different subset. The server is one Node process (ADR 0003) that already runs ffmpeg for the subtitle sync (ADR 0044) and has ffprobe's facts for every file in `media_files` (ADR 0040).

What has to be decided: how a file reaches a client that can play it as it is, how one that can't gets something it can, how a seek works in each, and who may load the media.

Alternatives:

- **Transcode everything to one format.** Simple, and wasteful: a 4K HEVC file played on a TV that decodes HEVC would be made again for nothing, at a cost in quality and in the server's processor.
- **Progressive transcodes over one HTTP response** (ffmpeg writing fragmented MP4 to the socket). No seeking past what's been made without restarting, and no switching between variants.
- **DASH.** Equally standard, but Apple's players don't play it, and HLS in fragmented MP4 now carries the same codecs (RFC 8216 § 3.3; Apple's HLS authoring specification).

## Decision

- **The decision, in order:** each play request carries the client's device profile ([ADR 0052](0052-device-profiles.md)). `@highsea-tv/stream` compares it with the version's streams and picks:
  1. **Direct play** when the container, every stream the person picked, and the bitrate all suit the client.
  2. **Direct stream** when the picture suits but the container or the audio doesn't: the video is copied, the audio converted, into HLS.
  3. **Transcode** otherwise: the video is made again, at the remote ceiling's bitrate when the person is away from home (Settings › Playback, [playback-settings.md](../product/playback-settings.md)).

  Each step writes its reasons as it goes, as the quality decision's `DecisionReason`s do (ADR 0031), in i18n messages, so Now playing ([now-playing.md](../product/now-playing.md)) and the tests say why.
- **Direct play** serves the file with byte ranges (`Range`, `206 Partial Content`; [RFC 9110 § 14](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110#section-14)). The file never moves or changes.
- **HLS** for the rest ([RFC 8216](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8216)): a multivariant playlist, a media playlist per variant, and fragmented MP4 segments (§ 3.3), with the target durations and keyframe spacing [Apple's HLS authoring specification](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/http-live-streaming/hls-authoring-specification-for-apple-devices) asks for, so AVPlayer plays it unchanged. The media playlist lists every segment from the start (`#EXT-X-PLAYLIST-TYPE:VOD`), since the film's length is known; the segments themselves are made when asked for.
- **Segments on demand:** ffmpeg runs as a child process ([Node's `child_process`](https://nodejs.org/api/child_process.html)), never in the server's thread, writing segments with its HLS muxer ([ffmpeg-formats › hls](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-formats.html#hls-2)) a few ahead of the player. A request for a segment far from what's being made restarts ffmpeg at the nearest keyframe before it. A session that's idle stops its ffmpeg.
- **The cache:** segments live under Settings › Playback's folder, kept while a film plays and a while after, removed oldest first, from films nobody is watching, when the cache passes its limit. Trickplay tiles are kept per film in the data folder and don't count.
- **Web** plays HLS through [hls.js](https://github.com/video-dev/hls.js) over Media Source Extensions where the browser has no native HLS; Safari and Apple's native players play it themselves.
- **Who may load media:** a media element can't send headers, so `/stream` takes a short-lived signed token, scoped to one person, session and file, issued by the API to someone whose session or device token may see that library. It's the calendar feed's pattern (ADR 0045), with a lifetime of hours, not months.
- **Positions** are reported over HTTP (`POST /api/v1/items/{id}/progress`), and other devices hear of them over the existing SSE stream (`watch.updated`). `/ws` stays reserved for SyncPlay and remote control, which come later.
- **Live sessions** are the runtime's: who, which device, title and version, the decision with its reasons, bitrate and position. They feed Now playing, and Stop ends a session's token and ffmpeg and tells the client why.

## Consequences

- Most plays at home cost the server nothing beyond reading the file, since direct play comes first.
- A transcode's quality and cost depend on the host. 4.3 adds hardware acceleration; until then transcodes use the processor, and Settings › Playback says so.
- Seeking far ahead in a transcode waits for ffmpeg to restart and make the first segment: a second or two on a processor. "Getting it ready" ([player.md](../product/player.md#the-streams-states-checkpoint-40b)) covers the first play.
- `packages/stream` is new, framework-free like the other domains, with subpaths as ADR 0036 sets them. Its tests build ffmpeg's command lines as snapshots, check playlists against RFC 8216's rules, and run real ffmpeg where it's present, skipping and saying so where it isn't, as ADR 0044's tests do.
- ADR 0003 gets a dated section when `/stream` stops being reserved.
- Left to settle at 4.1, from the docs: the segment length (Apple's specification recommends 6 s), how far ahead ffmpeg runs, when a film counts as watched, and the token's lifetime.

## 2026-10-27: what building it settled (checkpoint 4.1)

Built in 4.1a (the decision, direct play, tokens and sessions) and 4.1b (HLS, subtitles and trickplay), in `packages/stream` (`decision`, `sessions`, `hls`, `trickplay`), the runtime's `playback.ts` and `trickplay.ts`, and `apps/server/src/stream/`. What the draft left open, and what changed:

- **Tokens** last 6 hours, renewed by each progress report, and end with their session (the plan's answer 4). They're an HMAC over the session, person and file with a key made at boot, so a restart ends every stream; the client asks to play again.
- **Watched** is past 90% of the film's length (answer 3; ADR 0054).
- **One variant per session** (answer 7), chosen from the profile and the remote ceiling. A change of quality is a new session at the same position. The multivariant playlist names `CODECS` only when both streams are made again, since a copied stream's exact codec string isn't known without reading its bitstream.
- **Segments** are 6 s, as Apple's specification recommends.
  - A transcode forces a keyframe every 6 s (`-force_key_frames expr:gte(t,n_forced*6)`), so each segment is one file and the playlist is known before anything is made.
  - A direct stream can only be cut at the source's keyframes. ffprobe reads them once per file from the picture's packets and their flags, without decoding, and the runtime keeps them in `file_keyframes` by the file's size. A segment runs from one keyframe to the first at least 6 s later.
- **How ffmpeg is run**, from [ffmpeg-formats › hls](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-formats.html#hls-2) and [› mov, mp4](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-formats.html#Options-6):
  - `-hls_time 0` cuts a file at every keyframe, so a file's number is its keyframe's whichever run wrote it. Segments are served as their files joined.
  - `-copyts -start_at_zero` and the MP4 muxer's `movflags=+frag_discont` (through `-hls_segment_options`) write the film's own time into each fragment's `tfdt`. Runs started anywhere then make the same timeline and identical initialization sections, so a seek lands where the playlist says. Without `frag_discont` each run started its fragments at 0.
  - A transcode started after 0 also takes `-ss` and `-output_ts_offset` as output options, at the segment's start. Without them a copied audio track began at the source keyframe before it (3.6 s early on an HEVC film), and since hls.js takes its start from the earliest `tfdt`, subtitles were shown 3.6 s late.
  - `-hls_flags temp_file` makes a file appear only when it's whole.
  - ffmpeg runs 30 s ahead of the furthest segment asked for, and is then paused with `SIGSTOP` and continued with `SIGCONT`. A request more than 18 s past what's made, or before where the run started, restarts it there. A run nobody asks anything of for a minute stops.
- **The encoder** is libx264 `veryfast`, CRF 23 capped with `-maxrate` at the bitrate and `-bufsize` at twice it, High profile; AAC at 160 kb/s stereo, or 384 kb/s for 5.1 (answer 8). HDR made into SDR isn't tone mapped until 4.3 (answer 6), and the reasons say so. On an Apple M2 Max, a minute of HEVC 1080p took 6.0 s at 1080p (10× real time) and 3.6 s at 720p. The owner's NAS is measured with the beta (answer 10).
- **The cache** is the data folder's `transcodes/`, a folder per session, removed when the session ends and emptied at boot. Its size limit and Settings › Playback's folder are 4.3's.
- **Errors:** a run that fails logs its exit and its log's last 20 lines to System › Logs. The request answers 500 with "This film didn’t start."
- **Subtitles:** text tracks (embedded or sidecar SRT, ASS and WebVTT) are converted by ffmpeg to WebVTT once per session, at `/stream/subtitles/{token}/{track}.vtt`. For HLS they're also a subtitle rendition with one segment, the whole file ([RFC 8216 § 4.3.4.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8216#section-4.3.4.1)). Their cues are on the film's timeline, so no `X-TIMESTAMP-MAP` is needed. Image tracks have no URL until 4.3 burns them in. The offset is the client's.
- **Trickplay:** a frame every 10 s, 320 px wide, in 10 × 10 JPEG sheets, made from the keyframes alone (`-skip_frame nokey` with `fps`, `scale` and `tile`; [ffmpeg-filters › tile](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#tile)) at the lowest priority (`nice` 19). They're kept in the data folder's `trickplay/<file>/`, outside the cache. A 92-minute HEVC 1080p film took 16.7 s and made 6 sheets (3.5 MB). The `media.trickplay` job makes the newest film without them, one at a time, then queues itself again until the library has them all (answer 9). It runs after each import, and at boot when any are missing. A film that fails keeps its error until its file changes. Served at `GET /api/v1/items/{type}/{id}/trickplay` and `…/trickplay/{n}.jpg`.
- **Web:** hls.js 1.7.3 on the test page (`/stream/try`), which also plays Safari's native HLS. The web client takes it up in 4.2.

## 2026-10-28: limits, the cache, burn-in, the downmix and Now playing (checkpoint 4.3a)

Built in 4.3a in `packages/stream/src/hls/` (`args.ts`, `runner.ts`), the runtime's `playback.ts`, `playback-settings.ts` and `now-playing.ts`, and Settings › Playback and Now playing in the admin. What the plan left open, and what building it settled:

- **Settings › Playback** (`settings.get('playback')`): transcodes at once, 1 to 8 (default 3); the encoder, Faster, Balanced or Sharper (default Balanced); the most each stream is sent away from home, 4 to 40 Mbps or No limit (default 8 Mbps, 4.1's `REMOTE_CEILING`); the cache's folder and its limit, 2 to 2000 GB (default 20). They apply to sessions that start after a save. The hardware and tone-mapping settings are 4.3b's.
- **Transcodes at once:** a run that makes the picture again holds a slot while it runs; a direct stream's copy doesn't. Past the limit a run waits, first come first served, and gives up after a minute with a `limit` error (the plan's answer 7), which the player shows as Didn't start with its own line. hls.js waits up to 75 s for a segment's first byte (its `fragLoadPolicy`'s `maxTimeToFirstByteMs`), past the minute, so the 503 reaches the player. Safari's native HLS doesn't hand a segment's status to the page, so there it shows the general failure.
- **The encoder's three settings** are libx264's presets `superfast`, `veryfast` (4.1's, unchanged) and `faster` ([ffmpeg's H.264 guide](https://trac.ffmpeg.org/wiki/Encode/H.264), "Preset"); the CRF and the rate cap are unchanged.
- **The cache:** the folder defaults to `/config/transcodes` in the container (the data folder's `transcodes/`). A new folder must be a full path highsea can write, and is refused when it's inside, or holds, a root folder or the downloads folder, since highsea removes what it makes there. Sessions playing keep the old folder until they end. The limit is checked as pieces are written: past it, the pieces furthest behind the players go first, keeping the 60 s behind each player that it may seek back to, and never a piece ffmpeg may still be writing; a piece asked for again is made again. Clear removes every piece and stops nothing. `GET /api/v1/playback/status` reports the cache's use against the limit, with trickplay's size apart, and `playback.updated` follows each change.
- **Burn-in:** picture tracks of the film's own (PGS, VobSub, DVB) and ASS the client can't draw are burned in, which makes the run a transcode. A picture track is a second input to `overlay`, scaled to the source picture first and then scaled with it, so the overlay lines up whatever size is sent. ASS is taken out of the film once a session with the fonts the film carries, and drawn by `subtitles` (libass) with `fontsdir` ([ffmpeg-filters › overlay](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#overlay-1), [› subtitles](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#subtitles-1)). Every styled ASS track is burned in when the client can't draw ASS, since its styling is lost as WebVTT. A track that can't be read is logged and the film plays without it. A sidecar picture track isn't burned in yet.
- **The downmix:** 5.1 and 7.1 made into stereo use RFC 7845's matrices (§ 5.1.1.5, Figures 7 and 9) through `pan`, in ffmpeg's channel order. Unlike the plan, the LFE is kept, at the matrices' weight, as the RFC has it; the centre is −3 dB against the fronts and each surround is split between the sides, with the RFC's coefficients as it gives them. ffmpeg's own `-ac 2` doesn't scale the floating-point samples AAC takes (libswresample's `rematrix_maxval`), so its peaks clip; the plan's "quieter in the centre" was wrong, and the code comment says what it does. Other layouts keep `-ac 2`.
- **Speed:** ffmpeg's `-progress pipe:1` every second ([ffmpeg › Main options](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg.html#Main-options)). Its own `speed=` counts from the run's start, paused time included, so the runner measures its own, the film's seconds made per second over the last few reports while not paused. Unknown until a run has reported, which Now playing shows as "starting".
- **Stop** (`POST /api/v1/sessions/{id}/stop`, staff): ends the session's token and its run at once. The person hears `playback.stopped` over their events, and their player's next request about the session is a 404 whose details say who stopped it and the message, kept for a while after; the player shows its Stopped state.
- **The history** (`playback_history`): a row per session that ended with something watched, without addresses. A restart on the same device within 2 minutes, to change the sound or subtitles, joins the row before it. The `playback.prune` job removes rows past 30 days daily, safe to repeat (ADR 0022), and a person's rows go with them.

## 2026-10-28: the graphics chip (checkpoint 4.3b)

Transcodes run on a graphics chip once its check passes, and tone map HDR there or on the processor (ADR 0056). The runner (`hls/runner.ts`) reads the path and tone mapping for each run as it reads the limits, so a new check or setting applies to the next run. 4.1's segmenting is unchanged: the GPU encoders take their keyframes from `-force_key_frames` every 6 s, as libx264 does. A run that fails on the chip before its film ends starts again on the processor from the segment it had reached, its session marked fallen back for Now playing, and the runtime marks the path's check failed. Now playing's sessions name the path a transcode runs on, or the processor.

## 2026-11-17: a quick start, above all on a resume (phase 5, A1's finding; HT-46)

The owner's first films on the NAS took about 30 s to begin and 10 s to resume in the web app at home, and about a minute to resume on the phone over mobile data. Measured on a generated film (120 minutes, 23 GB, a keyframe about every 10 s) through a link held to 40 Mbit/s, ffmpeg itself was never the wait: it writes its first piece in 0.2 to 0.3 s from the beginning and from an hour in alike, since `-ss` before `-i` seeks by the container's index. The time went to four other things, and each is changed here. The plan's [log](../plans/phase-5-real-use.md#progress) has the numbers.

- **What takes a read of the whole file is read after the import, not at Play.** A direct stream's playlist needs every keyframe, which ffprobe lists from every packet of the picture: all 23 GB, counted. The film's first play waited for it. A subtitle of the film's own is spread through the file too, so converting one to WebVTT read the film again, once every session, beside the stream. The `media.ahead` job (`packages/runtime/src/ahead.ts`) now does both for each file after its import and for the library at boot, newest first, one file at a time at the lowest priority, as trickplay's job does: the keyframes into `file_keyframes` as before, and every text subtitle of the file's own in one pass into the data folder's `webvtt/<file>/`, named by the file's size and the stream. A play that comes first reads what it needs and shares a read under way. Sidecars are small files of their own and are still converted once a session.
- **A resume's playlist says where to start.** The media playlist carries `EXT-X-START:TIME-OFFSET=<seconds>,PRECISE=YES`: "By default, clients SHOULD start playback at this point when beginning a playback session" ([RFC 8216 § 4.3.5.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8216#section-4.3.5.2)). AVPlayer does, so the app no longer loads the film's first segment before seeking: the server ran ffmpeg three times for one resume (the resume's segment, then the first, then the resume's again) and sent the first segment for nothing. The native player and Safari's own HLS now seek only when the loaded item isn't already within a second of the start. hls.js takes the same place as its `startPosition`, as before.
- **A direct stream's first segments are short.** A player shows nothing until its first segment has arrived whole, and at a film's bitrate 6 s is tens of megabytes. Where a session starts, a segment begins at the keyframe before the start, and the segments beginning in the next 12 s are cut at the first keyframe 2 s on (`QUICK_SECONDS`, `QUICK_SPAN` in `hls/playlist.ts`). RFC 8216 only caps a segment's length at the target duration (§ 4.3.3.1), and Apple's 6 s is a recommendation for the stream as a whole, which the rest of the film keeps. A copied picture can only be cut at its own keyframes, so a film with one every 10 s gains nothing from this; a disc's picture, with one a second, starts on a third of the bytes. Transcodes are unchanged: their keyframes are forced every 6 s, and a shorter first segment there would need a second grid.
- **The log says where a start's time went.** Each play writes a few `info` lines to the `playback` source with its session id: `play session started` (the decision, the start position, the decision's time), `keyframes read` and `subtitle converted` (only when the play had to, with the time), `ffmpeg started` (the run's number, the position and piece it started at), `first segment ready` (ffmpeg's time to it, the playlist's time, the time since the session opened) and `first segment served` (the wait, the send, its bytes, the time since Play). A second `ffmpeg started` in one start is a player that asked for somewhere else.

`scripts/sweeps/start-times.ts` measures a start and a resume in the web player and in AVPlayer (`avplayer.swift`) against a generated film, through a proxy held to a link's rate.

**Keyframes from the file's index (HT-48).** Read from every packet, a library's keyframes are a read of every film: hours of disk work for the owner's 1,280 films on spinning disks after an update. ffprobe has no option that prints the demuxer's index ([ffprobe's documentation](https://ffmpeg.org/ffprobe.html): `-show_packets`, `-show_frames` and `-read_intervals` all read packets; `-skip_frame` is a decoder's option and still reads them), so highsea reads the two indexes itself (`packages/stream/src/hls/keyframe-index.ts`), a few fixed-size fields in a tree of sized elements, with no dependency:

- **Matroska:** the `Cues` element, "a temporal index" ([RFC 9559 § 4.5](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9559#section-4.5)), found among the Segment's first elements or through the `SeekHead`. "For each video track, each keyframe SHOULD be referenced by a `CuePoint` element" (§ 22.1), and a `CueTime` is in Segment Ticks, "timestamp in nanosecond = element value * TimestampScale" (§ 11.1.2). mkvmerge's default is a cue for every I frame of a video track ([`--cues`](https://mkvtoolnix.download/doc/mkvmerge.html): "The default is 'iframes' for video and subtitle tracks"), and ffmpeg's muxer adds one for every video keyframe when it can seek its output (`mkv_write_packet_internal` in `libavformat/matroskaenc.c`); written to a pipe, it writes no Cues.
- **MP4 and MOV:** the sync sample table, `stss` (ISO/IEC 14496-12 § 8.6.2), with each sample's time from `stts` (§ 8.6.1.2), `ctts` (§ 8.6.1.3) and a single edit's `media_time` (`elst`, § 8.6.6), as ffmpeg's mov demuxer reads them, so its packets' keyframe flags are this same table. A file without `stss`, a fragmented one (`mvex`, § 8.8.1) or one with more than one edit is left to the full read.

"SHOULD" isn't "MUST", and a direct stream's piece numbers follow every keyframe (`pieceStarts`), so an index missing one would serve the wrong pictures. Before it's used, ffprobe reads the film's own packets in three windows with `-read_intervals` ("ffprobe will seek to the interval starting point, and will continue reading from that"), and their keyframes must be the index's over the same stretch: from the file's start through the second keyframe (nothing before the first), one gap in the middle (none between two of the index's; a writer indexes the whole file the same way), and from the last keyframe to the file's end (the index covers the film's length). An index that's missing, can't be read, has fewer than four keyframes or disagrees anywhere falls back to the full read, as before, and the log's `read ahead of the first play` line says which (`from: index` or `packets`, and why). A play that comes before the job reads the same way.

**Subtitles in a job of their own.** A text subtitle of the film's own still needs the whole file: its blocks are spread through every Cluster, and Cues point at Clusters, each seconds of the whole film (§ 22.1), so ffmpeg demuxes it all. Converting in the same job held each film's keyframes behind a full read, so the conversions moved to `media.ahead.subtitles`, which `media.ahead` queues once no file is left without keyframes. A library's keyframes are now read first, seconds a film; its subtitles follow, newest first, one film at a time at the lowest priority, and a play that comes first still converts its own and shares a conversion under way. Converting only on demand was the other choice; it would bring back a wait of minutes the first time a subtitle is turned on, which HT-46 removed.

`scripts/sweeps/keyframe-times.ts` measures both reads on start-times' film, in MKV and MP4: the time, the bytes (through a file server, as HT-46 counted them) and whether the keyframes match. On the 120-minute film (keyframes 10 s apart), from the Mac's SSD: MKV 6.8 s and 23,039 MB from every packet, 0.48 s and 142 MB from the index; MP4 8.1 s and 23,043 MB, 0.38 s and 198 MB; the same 720 keyframes each way.

## 2026-11-18: subtitles in segments, in step with the picture (phase 5; HT-49)

A text subtitle of the film's own was one WebVTT file for the whole film, so turning one on needed the whole film read: once by the `media.ahead.subtitles` job after the import (HT-48), or by the first play that came before it. For a Matroska film that's every byte, about 2.5 minutes for a 23 GB film at a NAS disk's 150 MB/s, beside the stream's own read. A stream's subtitles now come in segments from the read its runs already make, so nothing reads the film for them.

- **What the documents ask.** "Each WebVTT Segment MUST contain all subtitle cues that are intended to be displayed during the period indicated by the segment EXTINF duration", each with its whole time even where part of it is outside the segment ([RFC 8216 § 3.5](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8216#section-3.5)), and the header carries `X-TIMESTAMP-MAP`, which § 3.5 says SHOULD be there and Apple's [HLS authoring specification](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/http-live-streaming/hls-authoring-specification-for-apple-devices) (5.3) makes a MUST. Its 8.22 asks every rendition to have "segment boundaries at the same points in time" as the picture, and its 5.5 a subtitle playlist "for the entirety of the main content". hls.js reads the same playlists ([its API](https://github.com/video-dev/hls.js/blob/master/docs/API.md), `enableWebVTT`), though highsea's web player keeps it off and loads the cues itself (below).
- **The runs write them.** Each run adds an output per text track of the film's own: `-map 0:<i> -c:s webvtt -flush_packets 1 -f webvtt <file>`, after the stream's own output, which still leaves subtitles out ([ffmpeg's synopsis](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg.html#Synopsis): "any number of output files"; `flush_packets`, [ffmpeg-formats](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-formats.html#Format-Options)). ffmpeg muxes each output apart, so the picture never waits on a sparse subtitle stream, as it would in one output for up to `max_interleave_delta`'s 10 s; on the generated film with `-re`, the first piece came at 10.14 s with the subtitle output and 10.05 s without. `-copyts` keeps the cues at the film's own times, the same times as the picture's fragments, so `X-TIMESTAMP-MAP=LOCAL:00:00:00.000,MPEGTS:0` maps them as they are.
- **Segments are cut from what's been read** (`packages/stream/src/hls/webvtt.ts`, `subtitleSegment` in `runner.ts`). The rendition playlist `subs/<track>.m3u8` lists the picture's segments with the same durations, at `subs/<track>/<n>.vtt`. A segment is every cue that shows in it, from every run's file, each once and in order. It's answered once the runs have read from 10 s before it to its end: a run counts as read up to the start of its last whole piece, a piece behind what it's writing, or the film's end once it exits there. It waits up to a minute, then answers 503 with `Retry-After` ([RFC 9110 § 15.6.4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110#section-15.6.4)). A subtitle never starts, moves or holds up a run: a seek or a resume starts the picture's run, and the subtitle follows it.
- **The cue already showing where a run starts.** Matroska's demuxer seeks to the picture's keyframe and drops every block before it, whatever its track (`matroska_read_seek` sets `skip_to_timecode`, which `matroska_parse_block` drops blocks against, in `libavformat/matroskadec.c`), so a run started at an hour never writes the line begun a second before. MP4's demuxer seeks each track on its own and keeps it. Once a run that starts past the beginning has its first segment out, a second ffmpeg reads only the subtitles of the 10 s before its start (`-ss <start − 10> -t 10 -copyts`, `leadInArgs`), a few megabytes, and that stretch counts as read. A line that began more than 10 s before a seek and still shows is missed there; such lines are rare, and the next segment has the lines after it.
- **The players.** The session's `url` for a text track of a stream is now the rendition playlist. A shared loader (`packages/native/src/subtitle-cues.ts`, with its rules in `@highsea-tv/client/player`) loads the playlist, then the segment showing and those starting in the next 30 s, each once, and asks again 2 s after a 503. It merges their cues, keeping one of each cue that's in two segments. The phone and the TV draw them as before. The web no longer has a `<track>` of a whole file: the cues are laid as `VTTCue`s on a text track the page adds (`addTextTrack`), styled by the same `::cue`, with the person's timing and the line's height laid on each cue as before, so nothing the player shows changes. AVPlayer reads the rendition itself when one is selected in it (`avplayer.swift`'s `legible`).
- **Sidecars and direct play.** A sidecar is a small file of its own: it's converted once a session as before and cut into the same segments. A file played as it is (MP4, MOV, WebM, by `profiles`) has no runs, so its subtitle stays one WebVTT file at `/stream/subtitles/<token>/<track>.vtt`, converted when first asked and kept. For MP4 and MOV that read is the subtitle's samples alone, found by the sample table (ISO/IEC 14496-12 § 8.7): 1.0 MB of a 41 MB test film by ffmpeg's own count, against all of it for the same film in Matroska. WebM is Matroska, so its subtitle still takes the whole file.
- **`media.ahead.subtitles` is narrowed to WebM files.** It was there so a play never waited for a whole-file read. A stream no longer needs one, and an MKV never plays as it is, so converting a library's MKVs read every film for nothing. It now converts only WebM files (`readWholeForSubtitles` in `ahead.ts`), the one case where direct play still needs the whole file read. Nothing a play had before is lost, so it's narrowed, not removed.

`scripts/sweeps/start-times.ts` measured it on its generated film (120 minutes, 23 GB, a text subtitle of its own) from the Mac's SSD through 40 Mbit/s, before and after, with the subtitle on. The first frame doesn't wait on a subtitle in any player, before or after: web 8.33 s → 8.33 s from the start and 8.64 s → 8.63 s on a resume at an hour, AVPlayer 7.38 s → 7.45 s and 14.85 s → 14.94 s. Before, every one of those plays converted the whole film's subtitle (`subtitle converted`, 1.8 s from the SSD, a read of all 23 GB, so minutes from a NAS disk), and its first line waited for it unless the job had been first; after, no play reads the film for a subtitle: the web asks for its first segments 10 to 20 ms after Play, and the app has the line at the start in hand after 0.2 s from the start (1.9 s before) and 1.3 s on a resume (1.9 s), where the 10 s before the start are read in 0.9 s. With the rendition chosen in AVPlayer itself, its first line came at 15.9 s, after its first frame, as before (15.8 s).

## 2026-11-18: a copy's run starts at its own keyframe, without an open GOP's leading frames (phase 5; HT-56)

A resumed MKV film failed in the web player ("This film didn't start") and showed no picture in AVPlayer. Two faults, both at the start of a run that copies the picture, so both on a resume (HT-46 made a resume's first run start past 0) and on a seek that starts a run:

- **The run started one keyframe early.** A copy's input `-ss` was a millisecond past the piece's keyframe. ffmpeg's demuxer seeks `3*AV_TIME_BASE / 23` before that when the format seeks by decode time and a stream has B-frames (`dts_heuristic` in [fftools/ffmpeg_demux.c](https://github.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/blob/release/7.1/fftools/ffmpeg_demux.c)), as Matroska does, and 7.1's Matroska seek lands a little earlier again, so the copy began at the keyframe before ("When doing stream copy … [the part before the seek point] will be preserved", [ffmpeg § Main options](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg.html#Main-options)). Every piece then held the GOP before its own, its times not the playlist's. The seek now goes halfway to the next keyframe (`copySeek`), and `-noaccurate_seek` keeps a sound track the run transcodes from being trimmed to that point ("useful e.g. when copying some streams and transcoding the others", [§ Advanced options](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg.html#Advanced-options)).
- **An open GOP's leading frames.** A Blu-ray's picture has open GOPs: the keyframe Matroska marks is an I-frame that isn't an IDR, and the B-frames after it in decode order show before it and refer to the GOP before. At a run's start that GOP isn't there; Chrome's decoder on macOS stops (`PIPELINE_ERROR_DECODE`) and AVPlayer draws no picture. A copy now drops the packets that show before its first, with the noise bitstream filter's `drop=lt(pts\,startpts)` ([ffmpeg-bitstream-filters § noise](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-bitstream-filters.html#noise), there since 5.1); with only `drop` set it changes nothing else. Later GOPs keep theirs.

The seed's clips are `ultrafast`, closed GOPs without B-frames, which is why no test met either. The playback sweep now imports a 720p Matroska film with B-frames, open GOPs, two sound tracks, its own subtitle and a PGS track, saves a place between two keyframes and resumes it: before, it showed the owner's screen; after, it plays.

What's left: a seek that lands in a segment's second or later piece of an open-GOP film. Chrome starts decoding at that piece's I-frame, whose leading frames refer to the piece before, which it has but didn't decode, and stops the same way. Every segment would have to start without leading frames, which a bitstream filter can't do for each piece; it's a finding for Phase 5's log, not this change.

## 2026-11-18: each piece keeps its keyframe's own time, and seeks in Chromium's software decoder (phase 5; HT-59)

The section above left a seek within a play of an open-GOP film open. Two separate things were found under it.

- **Every piece showed its keyframe three frames early.** The HLS muxer writes its fMP4 segments through movenc in DASH mode, where movenc sets each fragment's first sample's presentation time to where the last fragment ended, so a `sidx` lines up (`mov_write_single_packet` in [libavformat/movenc.c](https://github.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/blob/release/7.1/libavformat/movenc.c)). With B-frames that first sample is the keyframe, and its leading frames show before it, so each piece put its keyframe ahead of them: a wrong picture order at every keyframe, in every player. HLS reads no `sidx` ([RFC 8216 § 3.3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8216#section-3.3) names only `moof`/`mdat`), and with `skip_sidx` movenc writes none and keeps the film's own times ([ffmpeg-formats § mov, mp4, ismv](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-formats.html#Options-6)). The segment options are now `movflags=+frag_discont+skip_sidx`, Jellyfin's flags (jellyfin/jellyfin#17501).
- **Chromium's software decoder still fails a seek onto an open GOP's I-frame.** Since Chrome 149, Chromium's MP4 parser marks an I-frame that isn't an IDR as a keyframe when its SEI recovery point says it's whole (`recovery_frame_cnt` 0), so MSE may start a seek there (`kMediaSourceSeiRecoveryPointKeyframe`, `media/formats/mp4/mp4_stream_parser.cc`). The leading frames after it refer to the GOP before; Chromium's FFmpeg decoder runs with `AV_EF_EXPLODE` and stops on them (`PIPELINE_ERROR_DECODE`). Chrome's hardware decoders (VideoToolbox on a Mac), Safari, Firefox and AVPlayer play the same seeks. It meets Playwright's Chromium, Chrome on Linux without a hardware decoder, and Chrome with hardware decoding off.

Not chosen, with their costs:

- **Rewriting each piece** (drop a segment's first leading frames, give the keyframe their time, and mark later pieces' recovery points as not whole). It passed nearly every seek in five browsers, but one buffered sequence in Chromium still failed for a reason not found, and editing sample tables by hand is a risk across a whole library.
- **Starting runs and seek targets only at an IDR** lands a seek up to a GOP early, and doesn't help a seek within what's already buffered, where the browser picks the piece.
- **Re-encoding a run's first GOP** helps only at a run's start, for the same reason.
- **Transcoding open-GOP H.264 for Chromium** always plays, at the NAS's CPU for those films in Chrome. It's the fallback if the owner meets this in real use; the decision would need to know the browser's engine and the film's GOPs.

The playback sweep's open-GOP film now seeks right after its resume to a keyframe, on between two, back between two, back before the run's start and far back, in Firefox; Chromium skips those seeks and says why in the script. A test reads a later piece of a copy through ffmpeg and checks its keyframe keeps its own time, after its leading frames.

The iPhone's QuickTime logo on a resume (the section above) didn't reproduce on the iPhone 17 Pro simulator (iOS 26): a probe app with AVPlayer played the open-GOP H.264 film, HEVC Main and HEVC Main 10 from the start and resumed at 92 s, with and without `skip_sidx`, frames drawn every second and nothing in the error log. It waits for the film's stream list and a report from the NAS.

## 2026-11-18: the init is whole when it's sent, and every failed start is reported (phase 5; HT-64)

The iPhone failed most resumes of a direct-streamed film with `CoreMediaErrorDomain error 1718449215`, the four characters `fmt?`, which is Core Audio's and Core Media's "unsupported data format" (`kAudioFileUnsupportedDataFormatError`, [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/audiotoolbox/kaudiofileunsupporteddataformaterror)). It happened for HEVC with E-AC-3 and for H.264 with AAC, and once the same start showed AVPlayer's QuickTime logo with the sound playing and no error. The server's log was the same for every attempt.

**The cause** was the session's header. A run's `init.mp4` is copied to the session's `header.mp4` once the run's first piece is out (`packages/stream/src/hls/runner.ts`, `copyHeader`). Node's `fs.copyFile` is libuv's `uv__fs_copyfile` ([src/unix/fs.c](https://github.com/libuv/libuv/blob/v1.x/src/unix/fs.c)), which on Linux opens the destination with `O_CREAT`, then sets its owner and mode, and only then writes the bytes (FICLONE or `sendfile`). So the header exists, empty, for a moment. A player's request for `init.mp4` that came before the first piece waited for it; the same `settle()` that woke it started the copy, and `init()` then trusted `existsSync(header)`. The server stats the file for `Content-Length` (`sendFiles` in `apps/server/src/stream/index.ts`), so the player was sent 0 bytes, or part of the header. A resume asks for the init while its run is still starting, which is why resumes failed and fresh plays mostly didn't, and why it came and went.

**The fix:** the header is copied to `header.mp4.part` and renamed into place. POSIX makes rename atomic ([rename(2)](https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9799919799/functions/rename.html)), so `header.mp4` exists only whole. A test makes the copy behave as libuv's does on Linux (the file first, empty, its bytes 40 ms later) and asks for the header before the first piece. It got 0 bytes before the fix and the whole header after.

**What it does to a player:**
- **The iPhone simulator (iOS 26.5) with the app:** a resume sent an empty init fails with exactly `CoreMediaErrorDomain error 1718449215`, after fetching four segments.
- **AVPlayer on the Mac:** an empty init leaves the item ready with only its sound track enabled and no frame drawn, the logo's symptom. A partial one (25 to 95%) fails at once with -12894.

**The apps:**
- **Each failed session is reported.** A failed play is reported once per session, not once per run of the app: three failed starts in a row had left one report.
- **A failed stream ends its session at once,** so the server stops its ffmpeg.
- **The server logs each failed play's report** in the playback log (`a player said a play failed`, with the session). A start can fail after its first segments, whose end isn't "early" by `play session ended early`'s rule.
- **No picture counts as a failed start.** The phone and the TV count a start that plays 8 s of the film without a frame drawn (expo-video's `onFirstFrameRender`, from `AVPlayerViewController.isReadyForDisplay`) as failed. AirPlay is excepted. This catches a start that plays sound under AVPlayer's logo and says nothing.
- **AVPlayer's own account goes in the message.** expo-video's patch adds the underlying errors and the item's [error log](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avplayeritem/errorlog()) and [access log](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avplayeritem/accesslog()) to the failure's message. For an item that fails before it's ready they're empty, as they were for the empty init.
- **Nothing is left under the failed screen.** The picture and the subtitle line go under a stream's message, on every client: a failed item's picture is AVPlayerViewController's play glyph.

**Not changed, though Apple's [HLS authoring specification](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/http-live-streaming/hls-authoring-specification-for-apple-devices) asks for them:**
- A direct stream's `CODECS` attribute (9.1, MUST).
- Segments that start with an IDR frame where `EXT-X-INDEPENDENT-SEGMENTS` is written (7.4, 9.11).

AVPlayer plays these streams once the header is whole, so they wait for a finding of their own.
