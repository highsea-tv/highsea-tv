Written for highsea 0.2.0-beta.2.

**Now playing** shows who is watching what right now, how each stream is sent, and why. It's in the sidebar after **Activity**, with a count while anyone is watching. Owners and Admins use it. Users never open the admin.

It has two tabs, **Now** and **Last 30 days**.

## Now

The top of the tab adds up what's playing: **Streams**, the Mbps **Sent in all**, the Mbps sent **Away from home**, and the **Transcodes** running.

![Now playing's Now tab: the totals, then a row for Sintel playing on a MacBook Pro by direct stream, with its progress, Sent as, From, Network and the reason it isn’t played directly](/images/admin/now-playing/now.png)

Each stream has a row with the film, the person and their device, and a badge for its state:

| State | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **Playing** | The film is playing. |
| **Paused** | The person paused it. |
| **Waiting** | A transcode is waiting for a free slot, because highsea is already making as many as **Transcodes at once** in [Settings › Playback](/settings/playback/) allows. If no slot comes free within a minute, the player says the server is busy. |
| **Can't keep up** | A transcode runs slower than the film, so the player keeps stopping to wait for it. |
| **Stopped** | Someone stopped it from this page. The row stays for a few seconds, then goes. |

Beside the state, a second badge says how it's sent:

- **Direct play**: the file as it is. The device plays it unchanged, which costs the server almost nothing.
- **Direct stream**: the picture as it is, with the sound or the container changed for the device.
- **Transcode**: the picture made again for the device, as it plays. This is the work that needs a capable server.

Under the progress bar, the row lists:

- **Sent as**: what goes to the device and its rate, such as "1080p · H.264 · AAC stereo · 9.4 Mbps".
- **From**: the version of the film it's made from.
- **Network**: **At home** or **Away from home**, with the device's address. Streams at home are never limited; away from home, they're held to the limit in [Settings › Playback](/settings/playback/).
- **Transcoded by**, for a transcode: what does the work and how fast, against real time. "The processor, 2.4× real time" keeps up easily. Below 1×, the row says **Can't keep up**.

When a stream isn't played directly, the reasons follow in plain words, such as the device not playing the file's sound format, or a subtitle that has to be drawn into the picture.

### Stopping a stream

**Stop** ends a stream at once. You can add a message, up to 200 characters, which their player shows with your name. They can start the film again from where they were.

![The Stop dialog: Stop Ines’s stream, with an optional message shown in their player with your name](/images/admin/now-playing/stop.png)

## Last 30 days

The second tab lists every film watched in the last 30 days, newest first: the film, who watched it, on which device, how it was sent (with "away" when it was away from home), how long they watched, and when. **All** means they watched it to the end. Choose a person in the menu to see only theirs.

A few things keep the list honest:

- Starting a film again a moment later on the same device, to change the sound or subtitles, adds to the same row instead of making a new one.
- A film opened and closed without playing isn't listed.
- Addresses aren't kept, only whether it was at home or away.
- A daily task, **Clear what was watched over 30 days ago** in [System › Tasks](/system/tasks/), removes older rows. When a person is removed, their rows go with them.

## On the dashboard

The **Now playing** widget shows the same streams in brief: each film with how it's sent or **Can't keep up**, the person and device, and the progress, with the Mbps sent in all in its header. It comes first on the dashboard until you change it; move or remove it from **Customize**, as described in [the dashboard's guide](/dashboard/customize/).

## Being told when someone starts watching

A notification connection can hear **Playback started**, with who is watching, which film, on which device and how it's sent. It's off for every connection until you tick it: see [events](/settings/notifications/events/).
