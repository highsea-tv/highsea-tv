Written for highsea 0.1.0.

Three places decide who gets in and how:

- **Settings › Users and devices**: the people on the server, their roles, the browsers they're signed in on, and API keys.
- **Settings › Security**: the rules for signing in, such as two-step verification, passkeys and single sign-on. Only the Owner sees it.
- **Your account**: your own name, password, two-step verification and passkeys. Everyone has one; open it from the sidebar.

## Roles

Everyone on the server has one of three roles.

| Role | What they can do |
| --- | --- |
| **Owner** | Everything, including Security, Backups, VPN, Download client and Migration. The person who ran setup. There is one Owner. |
| **Admin** | Runs the server: the library, downloads, indexers and settings. Sees people and devices, but can't invite or change anyone. |
| **User** | Watches in highsea's apps. Never opens the admin. |

Only the Owner invites people, changes their roles and removes them. An Admin opening **Settings › Users and devices** sees the same lists with the note "Only the owner can invite or change people."

## Invite someone

1. Go to **Settings › Users and devices** and choose **Invite someone**.
2. Enter their **Name**, as the household knows them.
3. Optionally enter their **Email**. If the server's mail is set up in [Connections](/settings/notifications/), the link is emailed to them as well.
4. Choose a **Role**: Admin or User. For a User, choose their **Libraries**: **All, now and later**, or pick from the list.
5. Choose **Create invite link**, then **Copy link** and send it to them.

The link works once, for 7 days. Opening it, they choose a username and a password of at least 10 characters, then **Finish my account**. If single sign-on is on, they can choose **Join with** your provider instead, which links their account to it.

Until they finish, they show as **Invited** in the list, with "Hasn't joined yet". From the **⋯** menu beside them:

- **Copy the invite link** shows the link again, or a fresh one if the old one has expired.
- **Cancel the invite** stops the link working. You can invite them again later.

## Change what someone can do

Choose **Edit** beside a person to change their **Role**.

- Admins see every library and need nothing ticked.
- A User also has **Libraries** and **What they can do**: **Ask for titles** and **Download for offline**, which apply in highsea's apps rather than the admin. Changing the role resets these to the role's defaults.

Making an Admin a User signs them out of the admin, and their API keys stop working.

The Owner's role can't be changed, and there's no way to hand the server to someone else in highsea 0.1.0.

## Reset a password

Anyone who has forgotten their password asks the Owner. In **Settings › Users and devices**, open the **⋯** menu beside them and choose **Make a password reset link**. Send them the link: it works once, for 7 days, and lets them choose a new password. It's emailed too when the server's mail is set up and they have an email address.

The Owner's own password can only be changed by the Owner, in **Your account**. Nobody else can reset it, so keep it somewhere safe, along with your recovery codes.

To change your own password, open **Your account**, enter your **Current password** and the new one, and choose **Change password**. Your other browsers are signed out.

## Remove someone

From the **⋯** menu, choose **Remove**. They're signed out everywhere and can't sign back in, and their watch history goes with them. This can't be undone; invite them again if you change your mind.

## Devices

The **Devices** tab lists every browser signed in to the server: the device, whose it is, the app and its version, the address it last came from, and when it was last seen. **Whose devices** narrows the list to one person.

**Sign out** ends a device's session at once; the person signs in again with their password. The Owner can sign out anyone's devices, an Admin only their own. The browser you're using is marked **This browser**; sign it out from **Your account** instead.

## API keys

Scripts and other programs that talk to highsea, such as Home Assistant or a backup script, use an API key rather than a password.

1. On the **API keys** tab, choose **New API key**.
2. Give it a **Name** after what will use it.
3. Choose its **Access**: **Read only** sees the library, queue and status; **Full access** can do everything an Admin can.
4. Choose **Make key**, copy it, and store it where the script can read it. It shows only this once.

Give each script its own key, so you can **Revoke** one alone. Revoking can't be undone: anything using the key stops working at once.

Admins see and make their own keys. The Owner sees everyone's, and can revoke any of them.

## Two-step verification

Two-step verification asks for a 6-digit code from an authenticator app after your password. It's only asked for in the admin; apps never ask for it.

To turn it on for your account:

1. Open **Your account** and choose **Set up** under **Two-step verification**.
2. Enter your password.
3. Scan the QR code with your authenticator app, or enter the key shown below it.
4. Enter the code the app shows and choose **Turn on**.
5. Save the 10 recovery codes. Each signs you in once if you lose your authenticator, and they show only now.

**Your account** shows how many recovery codes you have left. **New recovery codes** replaces them all. At sign-in, **Use a recovery code** takes one instead of a code from the app.

When someone has lost both their authenticator and their recovery codes, the Owner can choose **Turn off two-step** from the **⋯** menu beside them. Make sure it's really them asking.

## Passkeys

A passkey signs you in with your fingerprint, face or device PIN, with no password to type. In **Your account**, choose **Add a passkey** and follow your browser. Each one shows whether it's **Synced** across your devices or kept **On one device**, and can be renamed or removed.

Passkeys work only when the admin is opened at its HTTPS address or at `localhost`. [Settings › Network and remote access](/settings/network/) covers HTTPS. At sign-in, choose **Sign in with a passkey**.

## Settings › Security

Only the Owner sees this area. Changes take effect when you choose **Save**.

### Signing in

| Setting | What it does | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Require two-step for the owner and admins** | Owners and Admins must use two-step verification to open the admin. Anyone who hasn't set it up is asked to at their next sign-in, and **Turn off** disappears from their account. | On |
| **Allow passkeys** | Offers **Sign in with a passkey**, and lets people add passkeys in **Your account**. | On |
| **Stay signed in for** | How long a sign-in to the admin lasts: 1, 7, 30 or 90 days. | 30 days |
| **Trust a sign-in header from my proxy** | For Authelia, Authentik and other forward-auth proxies. The proxy signs people in and passes their username in a **Header**, `Remote-User` unless you change it. It only signs in existing accounts, and highsea believes it only from the trusted proxies listed in [Settings › Network and remote access](/settings/network/). | Off |

### Single sign-on (OIDC)

Lets people sign in with Authentik, Authelia, Keycloak or another OpenID Connect provider. It needs the server's external address, set in [Settings › Network and remote access](/settings/network/), so the provider can send people back.

1. In your provider, create a client for highsea.
2. In highsea, turn on **Offer single sign-on**.
3. Enter a **Button label**: the sign-in page shows "Sign in with" and this name.
4. Enter the **Client ID**, the **Issuer URL** (the provider's `https://` address) and, if your provider gives one, the **Client secret**. The secret is stored encrypted; leave the field blank to keep the stored one.
5. Copy the **Redirect URL** highsea shows into your provider, then **Save**.

Single sign-on doesn't create accounts. Each person joins through their invite link with **Join with** your provider, and from then on that provider account signs them in. Someone the server doesn't know is told to ask the Owner for an invite.

## Sign-in protections

highsea slows down anyone guessing passwords:

- After 10 failed attempts at one account within 15 minutes, passwords for that account are refused for 5 minutes.
- After 50 failed attempts from one address within 15 minutes, that address is refused for 5 minutes.
- Each sign-in allows 5 tries at a two-step code, and the code step expires after 5 minutes.

Passkeys and single sign-on keep working during a pause, so someone guessing can't lock you out. Restarting highsea clears the counts.
