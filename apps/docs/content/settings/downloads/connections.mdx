Written for highsea 0.1.0.

The **Connections** section of **Settings › Download client** sets how highsea's torrent client reaches other peers.

![The Connections section: Listening port 6881, Encryption Prefer, Downloading at once 3, Open the port with UPnP off, Connect to peers over uTP on, and an empty IP blocklist field](/images/admin/settings-downloads/connections.png)

| Setting | What it does | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Listening port** | The port other peers connect to, for TCP and UDP. From 1024 to 65535. | 6881 |
| **Encryption** | Whether connections to peers are encrypted: **Require**, **Prefer** or **Allow**. | Prefer |
| **Downloading at once** | How many torrents download at the same time, from 1 to 20. The rest wait in the queue. Seeding torrents don't count. | 3 |
| **Open the port with UPnP** | Asks your router to forward the listening port. | Off |
| **Connect to peers over uTP** | Connects to peers over uTP as well as TCP. | On |
| **IP blocklist** | A list of addresses highsea never connects to. | Empty (off) |

## Listening port

Peers find you on this port. If you publish ports rather than use host networking, the compose file publishes 6881 for TCP and UDP. When you change the port here, change both sides of those lines too, such as `"6882:6882/tcp"` and `"6882:6882/udp"`, then recreate the container.

With a VPN that forwards a port, highsea uses the forwarded port; see [Port forwarding](/settings/vpn/port-forwarding/).

## Encryption

Encryption hides the BitTorrent traffic from anything watching the connection, such as an internet provider that slows torrents down. It doesn't hide who you are; a VPN does that.

- **Require**: every connection is encrypted. Peers that can't encrypt are left out, so you may find fewer.
- **Prefer**: highsea tries encryption first, and falls back to a plain connection when a peer doesn't support it.
- **Allow**: highsea connects without encryption first, and tries encryption with a peer that refuses that.

Prefer suits most people.

## UPnP and NAT-PMP

With **Open the port with UPnP** on, highsea asks your router to forward the listening port, so peers can reach you. It tries NAT-PMP first, then UPnP. Your router needs one of them turned on.

It does nothing while a VPN is in use: forwarding a port on your home router doesn't help a tunnel. Forward the port with your VPN provider instead. Leave it off if you've forwarded the port on your router by hand.

## uTP

uTP is a way of talking to peers over UDP that gives way to other traffic on your connection, so torrents slow your browsing and calls less. highsea uses TCP with peers that don't support it. Leave it on unless you're chasing a connection problem.

## IPv6

highsea connects to peers over IPv6 as well as IPv4 wherever the network, and the VPN, allow it. There's no setting for it.

## IP blocklist

Enter either:

- an `http://` or `https://` address of a list, which highsea downloads once a day; or
- the name of a file in the config folder.

The list can be in P2P, DAT or P2B format, and gzipped. highsea keeps the last good copy in the config folder, so it applies straight away after a restart. If a new copy can't be downloaded or read, the one in use stays, and System's health list says why. Leave the field empty to turn the blocklist off.
